# Assessment Policy

Assessment checks practical ability, understanding, and the student's ability to explain their own work. Practical work is the main measure of learning.

## Marks

| Assessment | Marks |
|---|---:|
| Weekly practical work | 30 |
| Homework | 15 |
| Mid-course practical exam | 15 |
| Final project | 30 |
| Theory and viva | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Detailed marking criteria are in [Assessment Rubrics](../assessments/assessment-rubrics.md).

## Completion requirements

A student must meet all of these requirements to receive a course completion result:

- Attend at least 80% of the 40 sessions: **at least 32 sessions**.
- Submit the required weekly practical assignments and final project.
- Complete the mid-course practical exam and earn at least **9 out of 15 marks (60%)**.
- Present the final project and answer basic questions about the work.
- Earn at least **60 marks out of 100 overall**.

If a student misses a session or assessment because of an approved reason, the instructor should arrange a reasonable catch-up task or assessment where possible. Attendance and marks must be recorded separately.

## Assessment schedule

- Weekly practical work: collected and reviewed during each week.
- Homework: assigned during lessons and checked in the following class.
- Mid-course practical exam: at the end of Week 4.
- Final project: developed during Week 8 and presented on Day 40.
- Theory and viva: short checks across the course, with a final review.

## Fair assessment

- Explain the task, time limit, and marking criteria before students begin.
- Give students a chance to ask questions about the instructions.
- Assess working results and understanding, not copied code.
- Ask students to explain or make a small change to submitted code.
- Give useful feedback: one strength and one practical next improvement.
- Keep individual marks private.
- Apply the same rubric consistently to all students.
- Keep instructor answer keys and unreleased exam solutions outside this public student repository.

## Academic honesty

Students may use lesson examples and documentation as learning references. They must write, adapt, test, and understand the code they submit. Copying another student's complete work and presenting it as their own is not acceptable.
